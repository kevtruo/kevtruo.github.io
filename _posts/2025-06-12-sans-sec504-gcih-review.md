## SEC504: Hacker Tools, Techniques, & Incident Handling / GIAC Certified Incident Handler (GCIH)

The SANS Institute's SEC504 course, coupled with the certification GCIH, is SANS' most popular certification, almost by both practitioners and recruiters alike. With 
that said, the elephant in the room with practically any SANS course is the daunting cost associated with both the SEC504 course as well as an exam attempt for the GCIH 
certification. Naturally, with such a high initial cost, close evaluation of the course syllabus and general cost-benefit analysis is critical for anyone looking into 
taking the course. Everyone's perceived valuation of being GCIH certified is wildly different, and hopefully this review gives you further insight to the course and contributes to your decision.

**Course Style:** OnDemand / BACS Program

**Funding:** GI Bill

### Course Strategy

BACS students get 8 weeks to pass the certification. Being aggressive from the start to complete all lecture material, quizzes, and labs is essential. With 8 weeks in 
mind, I set a goal of completing all lecture videos in 4 weeks, giving myself the remaining 4 weeks to drill on labs. Most SANS certifications contain "CyberLive" 
questions, which are questions that test your ability and knowledge of command and tool usage in a VM. Although they do not constitute the bulk of an exam, they are 
heavily weighted in your overall exam grade. Keeping this in mind, I wanted to allot myself as much time as possible to really understand the commands I was using.

### Module Quizzes

Through OnDemand, you are given quizzes at the end of each module, which do not contribute to your GCIH Certification Exam grade (but does impact your grade for the SEC504 course if you are a BACS or MSISE student). There is no need to stress over these quizzes, however I found that they were great pulse-checks in the effectiveness of your index (more on indexing later) so treating these seriously is helpful. The structure and difficulty of the quiz questions are somewhat helpful in exam preparation, but the real value is testing your index's content. There is no limit to these quizzes, so take them as much as you can time-allowing, regardless of your comfort in the module.

### Indexing

I can't recommend u/habitsofwaste's "Book Life Hack" reddit post enough. Consolidating all books into one single binder is essential (borderline mandatory) for any GIAC certification. In my situation, not having to fight against a stack of separate textbooks and instead use a single point of reference using a binder is a hidden, but enormous time-saver. Time, although seemingly plentiful, is not on your side. Supporting the binder, and the most important tool for taking any GIAC exam is the index itself. Here, I gained inspiration from Lesley Carhart's "Better GIAC Testing with Pancakes" blog post. You do not want to spend time looking for an individual page, and so Lesley's strategy is to further compartmentalize books and modules visually through colored tabs. The method here is to quickly associate colors by book and module, and then isolate the individual page itself. During the exam, I found myself repeating the book color, module color, and then page number in my head as I flipped through pages.

### Improvements

I did not ace the exam in the slightest. Although a pass is a pass, here are some improvements that I took away.
- Drill more on the labs. The CyberLive questions, which are **heavily** weighted, are also the same questions that will require a greater understanding of the material rather than simply answering a question which can be directly pulled from a page in the textbooks. Usage of command-line tools to answer incident response related questions requires not just an understanding of the requirements of the question itself, but how to actually leverage the command-line tool in question to receive the answer. You'll find that you'll need to know the why, versus the how.
- Usage of module quizzes more. Mentioned previously, the usage of module quizzes to hone down the index even more doesn't hurt, and any additional time spent here is a net benefit.
- Index item names. Deciding on what to name subjects in each index line-item is possibly the most critical action done in the preparation process. The first word of each line-item is critical, and my mistake here was beginning some line-items with a verb. My suggestion here is to always start with a noun.

### Key Takeaways

Although I do not presently work in a traditional incident response role, the lessons learned from the SEC504 course gives me great context in what my organization's IR teams do, 
as well as a level of knowledge that allows me to ask the right questions to the right folks.
