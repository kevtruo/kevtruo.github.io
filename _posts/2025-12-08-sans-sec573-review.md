## SEC573: Automating Information Security with Python / GIAC Python Coder (GPYC)

In a time where there are untold amounts of coding bootcamps, YouTube courses, and free Python references, it shouldn't be any surprise that SANS offers its own Python-focused course as well. The difference here between other Python resources and SANS is the institute's ability to leverage its expertise-backed curriculum in SEC573, where the course blends instruction of Python fundamentals with practical application of Python in offensive and defensive security contexts. The intent to be a course targeted to security professionals is definitely apparent. In light of that, the course attempts to provide relevant material for both experienced Python coders and those that don't have a concept of Python. Factoring in the Python language's extensive resource library and 3rd-party support, SEC573 sits at an interesting position regarding use-case and applicability.

**Course Style:** OnDemand / BACS

### Course Strategy

In the traditional on-site course style, students undergo two days of instruction for Python fundamentals, and 3 days of focused study on Python in a cybersecurity context. For those that go this route, it's clear that there needs to be substantial prep-time for those brand-new to Python. In the OnDemand sense, 2 months are allotted for BACS students. Generally, 8 weeks allows you the ability to split up each course section into their own weeks. At this stage, it's critical that each week's content is respected, and any additional time that needs to be spent gets their due diligence.

For those that are taking SEC573 as a part of the BACS program, there is the BACS 3373 Introductory Python course that is taken before SEC573, both taught by the same instructor. If you're new to coding, it's essential during BACS 3373 that you solidify Python fundamentals, as the time being spent while taking SEC573 is majority spent on Python's application in a defensive/offensive security sense. Using BACS 3373 effectively will grant a large amount of breathing room come time for the GPYC exam.

### Exam Strategy

The GPYC exam is different from other GIAC certification exams in that there are no CyberLive questions. Due to there being no CyberLive questions, instead of the typical 4 hours GPYC only allots 2. With 75 questions, this only gives about 2 minutes per question. During the exam, it's important to be cognizant of time and use the free 15 question skips that you have tactically. Questions essentially require reading a block of code and interpret it correctly, and as such allow a fair amount of trick questions throughout the exam. To help with timing, my recommendation is to mark your position in the exam at the hour mark and adjust accordingly.

### Lessons Learned

The great thing about Python is that there's an immense amount of free and paid material that can prep anyone for SEC573. Using additional materials, I'd wager that I could have gotten an even higher score on my GPYC exam.

### Evaluation

The elephant in the room with taking any SANS course is the cost and the return-on-investment with the corresponding GIAC exam. Personally, I've never seen GPYC listed as a desired certification on job postings. However, I was able to immediately apply the knowledge gained from SEC573 into the workplace; something that I was unable to do with other popular courses such as SEC504. Being conscious of that, I rate SEC573 highly but due to GPYC's limited recognition I'd rate GPYC a fair bit lesser. Ultimately though, due to Python's vast library of resources it's possible to gain the same level of knowledge with other free and paid sources.
