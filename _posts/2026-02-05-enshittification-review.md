> Enshittification: It’s not just you―the internet sucks now. It’s been enshittified. That was no accident, and it’s not gonna fix itself. Here’s how we’ll disenshittify it so we can have a new, good internet.

"Enshittification" is a technology book that takes a case study and commentary approach to discussing the lifecycle of the modern Big Tech company. The book is separated into 3 parts: the history, the pathology, the epidemiology, and the cure. 

The book is authored by Cory Doctorow, a Canadian-British tech journalist and activist particularly focused on digital rights, who actually coined the term *enshittification*; detailing the growth and eventual degradation of modern software. The book is a bit of a refreshing read as Doctorow takes a casual approach in discussing the highly complex topics concerning the intersection of technology, monopolies, shrewd business tactics, and the maze of corporate legalities. You get the impression of not being lectured about theoretical concepts and theories on the economics behind the giants Google and Apple, but rather a conversation (maybe even a rant) of why things are they where they are, and what they could be. Without asserting credibility explicitly, Doctorow uses his own experiences working for digital rights organizations like the Electronic Frontier Foundation to really hone-in on the importance of battling against monopolistic maneuvers in the digital realm. With these experiences, Doctorow made it easy to understand the several concepts and processes at play.

Something I want to note is the book's setting (mid-late 2025), as we the reader observe the monopolistic nature of the major corporation at its current state and reflect against the mid-late 2025 time period of *Enshittification*. The book discusses the second Trump administration's actions regarding anti-trust and user-privacy in-detail, and for the context of the reader it's important to recognize that four, three, or even two years later post-publication may present a completely different corporate and political climate for the United States. As such, the return value of *Enshittification* will diminish overtime. Not to say that reading cover-to-cover isn't valuable at all; Doctorow identifies several key mechanisms in the enshittification process that will very likely manifest in tech companies for many years to come.

Overall, I'd rate *Enshittification* a 7.0 / 10.0. I liked the beginning of the book the most where Doctorow discusses enshittification within many of the public's beloved Big Tech companies and breaks the illusion poised by effective marketing. On the flip-side, act 3 was my least-favorite, where Doctorow seems the draw out the immorality of enshittification a bit long. For the prospective reader, *Enshittification* is a great value-add for anyone curious about the state of technology, and I personally found it to be an enjoyable read.
