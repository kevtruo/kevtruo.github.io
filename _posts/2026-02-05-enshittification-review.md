> Enshittification: It’s not just you―the internet sucks now. It’s been enshittified. That was no accident, and it’s not gonna fix itself. Here’s how we’ll disenshittify it so we can have a new, good internet.

"Enshittification" is a technology book that takes a case-study-and-commentary approach to discussing the lifecycle of modern Big Tech companies. The book is separated into 3 parts: the history, the pathology, and the cure.

The book is authored by Cory Doctorow, a Canadian-British tech journalist and activist particularly focused on digital rights, who actually coined the term *enshittification*; it details the growth and eventual degradation of modern software. The book is a refreshing read because Doctorow takes a casual approach to discussing highly complex topics at the intersection of technology, monopolies, shrewd business tactics, and corporate legalities. You get the impression that you are not being lectured about theoretical concepts or the economics behind giants like Google and Apple, but rather having a conversation (maybe even hearing a rant) about why things are the way they are, and what they could become. Drawing on his experience working for digital rights organizations like the Electronic Frontier Foundation, Doctorow hones in on the importance of pushing back against monopolistic maneuvers in the digital realm. Those experiences make the book's concepts and processes easy to follow.

Something I want to note is the book's setting (mid-late 2025), as the reader observes the monopolistic nature of major corporations in their current state and reflects on the mid-late 2025 time period of *Enshittification*. The book discusses the second Trump administration's actions regarding antitrust and user privacy in detail, and for context it's important to recognize that four, three, or even two years after publication may present a completely different corporate and political climate in the United States. As such, the return on *Enshittification* will diminish over time. That said, reading it cover to cover is still valuable; Doctorow identifies several key mechanisms in the enshittification process that will likely manifest in tech companies for years to come.

Overall, I'd rate *Enshittification* a 7.0 / 10.0. I liked the beginning of the book most, where Doctorow discusses enshittification within many of the public's beloved Big Tech companies and breaks the illusion posed by effective marketing. On the flip side, act 3 was my least-favorite section, where Doctorow seems to draw out the immorality of enshittification a bit too long. For prospective readers, *Enshittification* is a strong value-add if you're curious about the state of technology, and I personally found it to be an enjoyable read.
